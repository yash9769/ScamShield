ScamShield · UI Redesign01
The brief, and what was actually wrong

The app was dressed as a hacking tool

ScamShield's users are, disproportionately, the people scams actually work on — someone who just got a text about their bank and whose pulse is up. The old interface met them with a pulsing radar sweep, six shouted navigation labels and a risk score out of 100.

What the old Home spent its space on

A 140px pulsing shield inside an ambient radar sweep · a “SHIELD ACTIVE / ECOSYSTEM” badge that read the same after 0 scans and after 400 · a 2×2 grid of stat cards including “AVG RISK SCORE” · and a banner listing “Androguard + YARA”, “Zero dummy data”.

Roughly the entire first screen passed before anything the user could act on appeared.

What people actually open it for

One question, every time:

“Is this thing I just received safe?”

Everything on the screen now either answers that question, or gets out of the way of the thing that does.

Four rules the system is built on
1

Colour means something

Green, amber and red belong to verdicts and nothing else. When every card carries a cyan glow, a red “Scam” banner is just more colour. Neutrals do the structural work; one accent marks what is interactive.

2

Sentence case, real hierarchy

ALL-CAPS with +1.5 tracking is measurably harder to read and shouts at someone already anxious. Nineteen shouted labels were removed. Size, weight and colour carry hierarchy instead.

3

Stillness is a feature

Motion happens on state change, never on a loop. The pulsing CTA and the radar sweep are gone. A screen that never settles keeps taking attention without giving anything back.

4

Space over ornament

Glows, gradients and drop shadows removed until something broke. Depth now comes from one surface step and a hairline border.

Three colours are reserved

These never appear as decoration, section headers or flavour icons. Every place they are used is also labelled in words and paired with a distinct icon — roughly one in twelve men cannot separate red from green, which is precisely the distinction this app exists to communicate.

Larger text is tracked tighter, small text marginally looser, approximating an optical-size axis Flutter does not expose. The old theme layered extra positive tracking on top for “tactical” labels — that is the letter-spacing this redesign removes.

Space & radius

Everything is a multiple of 4 — which is what stops a layout looking hand-placed. Three radii plus a pill; more than that and nothing looks related.

What moved, and why it matters

The old result card opened with a coloured word, a “GEMINI AI” badge and “Risk Score: 87/100” — a measurement, a vendor name and a number nobody can act on, all above the advice. Someone reading this is often mid-decision about whether to tap a link or send money. The instruction now comes first; the score is a footnote. The provider label was also simply wrong much of the time — analysis falls back to Groq or to the on-device engine, and the footnote says so honestly.

Reach

The token names in AppColors were kept deliberately unchanged. Around five hundred references across twenty-odd screens point at them, so re-tuning the values restyles the entire app — including the fourteen screens not individually rewritten — without a single call site needing to change. Five screens got the full structural redesign on top: Home, Scan, History, Profile and the navigation bar.

What this document is, honestly

These are design renders built from the same tokens as lib/theme.dart — not screenshots of a running build. There is no Flutter SDK in the environment this was produced in, so the Dart could not be compiled or launched here. Every widget API used was checked against the real signatures in the codebase, and every file was verified to have the same structural balance as its committed baseline, but running flutter analyze and launching the app on a device is still the step that confirms it.

The obvious next move

A light theme. The system is already token-driven, so it is mostly a second ThemeData — but the fourteen un-rewritten screens hardcode light-on-dark colours, and flipping the default before those are migrated would leave unreadable text in places nobody would find until a user did. It is a follow-up with a real migration behind it, not a switch to flip.
